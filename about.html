About

As a geospatial analyst and interdisciplinary planner, I explore how spatial data and systems shape urban development and the way places change. I am persistently curious about how rules become physical environments, how hidden patterns in data influence real decisions, and how changes to a place affect the people connected to it. I approach cities as more than collections of infrastructure, but as environments that hold memory and belonging.

Through GIS and spatial analysis, I work to uncover conditions that may have always existed in the data but have not yet been clearly recognized. I am especially interested in using data for more than describing what has already happened. I want to anticipate future risks and connect environmental conditions with human consequences. I also aim to translate complex findings into recommendations that people can understand and apply.

My legal background deeply shapes how I approach this work. Before graduate school, I worked in legal settings and studied how regulation and institutional decisions affect individuals and communities. These experiences trained me to examine problems from the perspective of those with decision-making authority. They also taught me to consider the people who must live with the consequences. This perspective draws me to aging or overlooked systems that no longer serve people as they should. I aim to preserve what gives a place its integrity while using spatial evidence to support thoughtful and necessary change.
